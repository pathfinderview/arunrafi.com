01 · Store

The mainspring

The spring holds 40 hours of energy. Why not let it turn the hands directly?

Like a wind-up toy: wind it, let go, gone in seconds.

TryDrag the crown up to wind. Then lift the click.

Eureka 1

Wind it, then let it go.

A second, not forty hours. A spring stores energy. It can't keep time. The rest of the watch makes it spend that energy slowly.

02 · Divide

The gear train

How does one spring turn one hand per minute and another per hour?

Like bicycle gears, used to count.

TryTurn the seconds wheel once. Watch the minute hand.

Eureka 2

Turn the seconds wheel once round.

One turn of seconds = one minute. Gears count perfectly. But you set the speed. They have no idea how long a second is.

03 · Release

The escapement

So what decides how fast the wheels may turn?

Like a turnstile: the crowd pushes, one gets through per click.

TryBe the heartbeat. Tap once a second. Amber is you, blue is real time.

Eureka 3

Tick for 15 seconds.

Your seconds weren't equal.

The escapement turns a push into steps. But it's only as good as whatever rocks it.

04 · Beat

The balance wheel

What can rock the fork perfectly evenly, all day?

Like a swing: high or low, each swing takes the same time.

TryRelease both: one big swing, one small. Then move the regulator.

Eureka 4

Release both, count a few ticks.

Big swing, small swing, same beat. Further means faster. So the watch keeps time as the spring weakens. Move the regulator a hair and the beat changes. That's how watches are adjusted.

05 · Sustain

The handshake

Swings fade. What keeps the balance going for two days?

Like pushing a swing: one quick push at the bottom, then hands off.

TryStep through one beat. Then disconnect the spring.

Eureka 5

Disconnect the spring.

No push, and the swing dies. Each tick the spring gives the balance a tiny push back. That's why a stopped watch needs a shake.

06 · Together

The whole watch

Who's really in charge: the strong spring, or the tiny balance?

TryHold the balance. Hover for the loupe. Open the blueprint.

Eureka 6

Hold the balance.

Everything stops. The spring has the power. The balance has the say.

In one line

Spring stores → gears count → escapement releases → balance decides when.

About this model

Drawn to scale (1 mm = 25.2 px). Tooth counts and the 4 Hz balance are typical of a hand-wound calibre, not a copy of one. One clock drives every part; the ratios are exact. Tooth shapes, motion works and setting parts are simplified. The exploded view stacks dial and hands above the balance for clarity. Chapter 5 exaggerates friction. Sound is synthesised.
