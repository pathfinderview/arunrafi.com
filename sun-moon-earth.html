The Moon

Why the Moon changes shape

One story in nine pictures. Touch them all.

0

Look up tonight

This is the Moon tonight. Every night it looks a little different.

1

The Moon makes no light

It is a ball of grey rock. The Sun lights one half of it. Always half.

Drag the Sun.

2

We see it from the side

The Moon goes round us. From here we see some of its day, and some of its night.

Drag the Moon.

3

One trip takes a month

29½ days, from new Moon to new Moon. The word month comes from Moon.

4

So it is often out in the day

The Moon rises about 50 minutes later each day. Which part of the day it's up depends on its shape.

5

It always shows us one face

It turns once on every trip round, so the same side always faces us.

6

From the Moon, Earth changes too

Its shapes are the opposite of ours. Earth's light makes the Moon's dark part glow.

Drag the Moon.

7

And it isn't Earth's shadow

Earth's shadow points away from the Sun. The Moon can only reach it when it's full.

Drag the Moon to new and full.

8

Try it with a lamp

A dark room. One lamp. A ball. You are the Earth.

  1. Hold the ball up at arm's length, a little above your head.
  2. Turn slowly to your left.
  3. Watch it go new, half, full, half, new.

One lamp. One ball. You in the middle, turning.

About these pictures

Distances are squashed so everything fits. Tonight's Moon in chapter 0 and the calendar in chapter 3 are worked out in your browser from today's date, close to the day. Chapter 4 shows the sky as seen from the northern half of the world, facing south; in the southern half the shapes are flipped.

Sources: NASA Moon phases and NASA Moon facts.
