Why two bumps?

Three friends race to the Moon. The Moon pulls the closest friend hardest. So they spread out!

The Earth is full of friends. It gets stretched. A bump in front, a bump behind.

03

The Earth spins round.

Your beach goes round and round. Under a bump: water up! Between the bumps: water down.

Two bumps. So, twice a day.

Why the Moon beats the Sun.

The Sun pulls the Earth 180 times harder than the Moon does. So why does the Moon run the tides?

Tides are about the difference between the pull on the near side and the far side. That difference fades with distance cubed, and the Moon is 390 times closer.

The space pictures look down on the North Pole. The Moon is drawn ten to twenty times too close, and the bumps thousands of times too tall: on the open sea the real bump is under a metre, and coasts funnel it into the tides you see. Your beach is Marina Beach, Chennai. Scene 04 starts from today's real Moon.
