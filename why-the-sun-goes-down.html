The Sun

Why the Sun goes down

It never moves. Six pictures you can turn.

0

Every evening

It slides into the sea. Almost straight down.

1

But the Sun doesn't move

You stand on a ball that turns. Your ground swings up and hides the Sun.

Drag the Earth.

2

Why it feels the other way

Cut out your piece of the Earth. It swings up in front of the Sun. Standing on it, you see the Sun slide down.

Drag the ground.

3

It's the spin, not the lap

One spin is a day. One lap round the Sun is a year. In a day, the lap barely moves.

Seen from above the North Pole. Both turn anticlockwise.

4

Why it drops straight down

The Sun circles the Earth's axis. At the equator the axis lies flat along your ground, so the circle stands upright.

Drag yourself up the globe.

5

The lap moves it sideways. Slowly.

Earth leans 23° as it goes round. So through the year the sunset slides along the sea line.

6

Try it with a chair

A chair that spins. A lamp across the room. You are the Earth.

  1. Sit facing the lamp. That's noon.
  2. Turn slowly to your left.
  3. The lamp slides right and goes behind you. Sunset.
  4. Keep turning. It comes back on your left. Sunrise.
About these pictures

Sizes and distances are squashed so everything fits. Chapters 1 and 3 look down on the North Pole. Chapter 4 shows a day in March or September, when the Sun sets due west. Chapter 5 uses the place you picked in chapter 4, facing west; the day of the year starts on today.
